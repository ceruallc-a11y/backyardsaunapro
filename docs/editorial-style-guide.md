# Editorial Voice and Writing Standard

Use this standard for every article, guide, product comparison, newsletter, landing page, CTA, and customer-facing message.

## Core voice

Write like a knowledgeable person helping a homeowner make a real decision. Sound calm, direct, and useful. Do not sound like a manufacturer brochure, a generic AI summary, or a search-engine checklist.

Accuracy still comes first. A human voice must never soften an important limitation, hide uncertainty, or turn a manufacturer claim into an editorial fact.

## Sentence structure

- Keep related words close together. Put the subject, action, and object near one another.
- Prefer a direct sentence over a long chain of clauses.
- Break a crowded sentence into two when each idea can stand on its own.
- Use active voice when it makes responsibility clearer.
- Put the important decision or warning early in the sentence.

Example:

> The heater needs a compatible controller. Price both pieces before comparing quotes.

Avoid:

> When considering the heater, which requires a compatible controller that may be sold separately, buyers should take this additional expense into account when comparing the various quotes they have received.

## Rhythm and complexity

Vary sentence length. A short sentence can land a decision. A longer sentence can explain why it matters.

Use complexity when the topic requires nuance, such as electrical planning, evidence quality, or product limitations. Follow that detail with a plain-language takeaway.

Do not make every paragraph the same length. Do not start several consecutive sentences with the same phrase.

## Natural language

- Use contractions when they fit the page.
- Choose concrete words: `breaker size`, `room volume`, and `delivery access` are better than `important considerations`.
- Address the reader as `you` when guiding a decision.
- Use a relatable example only when it clarifies a real tradeoff.
- Avoid cliches, filler, hype, and canned transitions.
- Avoid empty openings about saunas being ancient, popular, luxurious, or transformative.

## Buyer usefulness

Every section should help the reader do at least one thing:

- understand a tradeoff;
- verify a claim;
- avoid a mistake;
- compare options;
- estimate cost or effort;
- decide what to do next.

Remove a paragraph when it does none of these.

## Evidence and trust

- Separate documented facts, manufacturer claims, editorial interpretation, and unknown information.
- Never invent testing, ownership, measurements, reviews, quotes, certifications, prices, or firsthand experience.
- Use current primary sources for specifications, safety instructions, standards, and legal or medical-adjacent claims.
- State meaningful uncertainty plainly.
- Keep affiliate and sponsorship disclosures visible and direct.
- Preserve the Sun Home protected-scope agreement in `docs/sun-home-protected-scope.md`.

## Final human-sounding edit

Before publishing, read the page aloud and ask:

1. Does each sentence connect naturally to the next?
2. Are related words and ideas close together?
3. Is there a healthy mix of short and longer sentences?
4. Does technical detail end in a clear takeaway?
5. Does any sentence sound like generic AI copy or manufacturer marketing?
6. Can a vague phrase be replaced with a concrete fact or action?
7. Is every claim supported and every limitation still visible?

If the page sounds polished but impersonal, revise it. If it sounds conversational but less accurate, restore the evidence and simplify again.
